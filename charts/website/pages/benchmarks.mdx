# Benchmarks

Every release from 2.5.8 on publishes the same measurement, the way it publishes
its changelog: the render bench run on the new release and on the one before it,
one after the other, on the same machine with nothing else running. Both builds
are made from their release tags, so each entry compares what you would install.

The bench draws a price chart with a volume pane in headless Chromium and times
three things at 10,000, 50,000 and 200,000 loaded bars, on the 2D canvas and the
WebGL2 backend:

- **Pan frame**: a mouse drag with about 200 bars in view, one bar per step.
- **Full zoom-out frame**: every loaded bar in view, one pixel of drag per step.
- **Tick with 10 studies**: a forming-bar update on a chart carrying ten studies,
  the frame that recomputes and repaints them.

Each step is timed from the input to painted pixels. A cell gives the previous
release's p95 and this one's, each the lowest of several runs, and how many times
faster or slower the step became; within 5% either way reads as the same.

Read the figures as a comparison within one entry. The absolute numbers belong to
the machine that measured them, and headless Chromium draws WebGL on a software
GL device, so the `webgl2` rows compare one build of the WebGL path with another,
not WebGL with the 2D canvas or with a real GPU. CI holds every change to separate
frame budgets on its own runner; the method, the budgets and why a fast result can
be trusted are in the
[performance notes](https://github.com/marketcalls/openalgo-charts/blob/master/docs/performance-notes.md).
The raw timings of every run are in
[`benchmarks/releases.json`](https://github.com/marketcalls/openalgo-charts/blob/master/benchmarks/releases.json).
