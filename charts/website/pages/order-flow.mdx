# Order flow and market profile

A candle tells you where price went. Order flow tells you who took it there. A
footprint chart opens each bar and shows how much traded at every price, split by
the side that started each trade: a buyer lifting the offer (the ask) or a seller
hitting the bid. Read that way, a bar shows where buyers or sellers pressed, where
their orders were soaked up, and where price moved through a level with almost
nothing traded.

Market profile steps back to the whole session. A TPO profile prints one letter at
each price for every half hour that traded there, so the shape of the day (where
it spent most of its time, its value area and its point of control) stands out at
a glance. A volume profile does the same with traded volume.

Every chart below runs the real `openalgo-charts/profile` tier in your browser on a
seeded, simulated tape, so each visit shows the same bars. Scroll over a chart to
zoom and drag to pan. The footprints share one tape of fourteen one-minute bars
from 09:15, so a bar named in one example can be found in all of them.

## Reading a footprint: bid by ask

Each column is one bar. Every row is a price, with the volume sold into the bid on
the left and the volume bought from the ask on the right, and a slim candle beside
it. The outlined row is the point of control (POC), the price with the most volume
in that bar. Wider rows group neighbouring prices together: try 2 or 4 points to see
a busy bar condense into a few readable rows.

## Imbalances and stacked imbalances

Imbalances compare diagonally, because at any moment the best bid sits one row below
the best ask: the ask volume at a price is weighed against the bid volume that stood one
row under it. An ask cell is a buying imbalance
when it is at least the ratio times the bid one row below it; a bid cell is a selling
imbalance against the ask one row above. Three or more in a row on one side form a
stacked imbalance, drawn as a bracket: a strong sign one side was in control.

A row inside a bar that never traded counts as zero volume, so the cell beside it
is compared with zero, and any positive volume against zero is an imbalance (new in
2.6.4). The 09:16 bar shows it: price jumped two points at a time on its way up,
leaving 24004, 24006 and 24008 untraded, and every traded row beside a gap lights up.

## One value per row: the display modes

When bid and ask side by side is more than you need, print one number per row.
**Delta** is ask minus bid, **Delta %** is that as a share of the row's volume, and
**Volume** is the total. **Delta-shaded volume** (new in 2.6.4) prints the row's volume
on a cell coloured by the sign of its delta and shaded by its size, so you read size
and direction together: a deep green cell was bought hard, a deep red one sold hard,
and a pale one was close to balanced. **Volume and delta** prints both numbers.

## Cell styles: heatmap, profile and ladder

The style changes only the drawing, never the numbers. **Heatmap** shades each cell by
its volume, **Profile** draws bars that grow out from the middle (bid to the left, ask
to the right) so a bar reads as a small histogram, and **Ladder** draws square,
high-contrast cells. Shading against the busiest row on screen, instead of within each
bar, makes the heaviest clusters on the chart stand out.

## Order flow signals

Each layer marks one pattern that traders look for, and each is off until you ask
for it. A **large-volume cell** (`bigOrderMultiple`) is a single cell several times the
average near the bar's high or low, often read as a large resting order, though the chart
only sees executed volume. **Absorption** bands rows near the high or low where both bid
and ask volume were heavy. **Multiple nodes** highlights a POC that two bars share. A
**delta flip** marks a bar whose delta turned after a run of the other sign at a
swing. **Divergence** boxes the delta label when it disagrees with the candle, such as
the up bar at 09:18 that closed with net selling. **Unfinished auctions** draw a
dash-dot line at a high or low that traded on both sides, a level price often
revisits.

## Zones and zone alerts

With zones on, stacked imbalances, shared POCs and absorption extend to the right as
price levels until price deals with them. `zoneKinds` picks which of the three to
draw and alert on, and `zoneLookback` lets zones start only in the newest bars, so an
old, crowded chart stays readable. `onZoneAlert` calls you once per zone when the
newest bar's range comes back to it after it became active (a jump across the zone counts,
as it would trigger a stop there), the moment to raise an alert
or play a sound. Zones touched in the bars a chart first loads are history, not news,
so they are not reported. Press Play to run the tape forward and watch the log.

<ZonesLiveExample />

## The statistics table

A table under the footprints gives each bar its numbers: the session letter (here one
per five minutes, so the fourteen bars run from A to C), ask and bid volume, delta
(ask minus bid), delta % (delta over volume), the highest and lowest the running
delta reached inside the bar, cumulative delta and cumulative delta per volume
(running totals across bars, the second a ratio between -1 and 1), volume and
cumulative volume. Signed rows are shaded green when positive and red when negative,
more strongly the larger the value.

## Volume profile of the visible bars

A profile against the left edge adds up every visible bar's volume at each price,
split into ask and bid, so you can see where the whole visible range traded most
without reading forty columns. It is rebuilt as you pan and zoom.

## Footprint colour themes

`FOOTPRINT_THEMES` (new in 2.6.4) holds six ready colour sets. Each is a plain options
patch you spread under your own choices, and covers cells, imbalances, candles, the
POC, the signal layers, the visible profile, session letters and the table. A theme
never sets the chart background: Daylight is made for a light chart and the other
five for dark ones, so this example sets a matching background as you switch.

## Trade size filter and lot display

A feed reports raw quantity, while traders often think in lots. `volumeDivisor` divides
every printed quantity, so the same bars read in lots or units without rebuilding.
`minTradeSize` on the aggregator counts only prints of at least that size, to see what
the larger participants did. Smaller prints are not thrown away: they still open their
row (it shows zero) and still set the bar's open, high, low and close, so each column
keeps its full range.

<TradeFilterExample />

## Market profile (TPO)

Each session is a column of letters: A for the first half hour, B for the second and
so on, one letter at every price that period traded. The widest row is the POC, the
shaded band is the value area (where 70% of the letters sit), and the line at the
left of each profile marks the initial balance, the range of the first hour. A lone
letter at an extreme, a single print, shows price moved through quickly. Colour by
value area or by volume to read the same day another way, or split the periods to see
how the day built over time.

<MarketProfileExample />

## Volume profile by session

A volume profile is the market profile's volume twin: a horizontal histogram of how
much traded at each price, with the POC and value area marked. Build one per day, or
one over the whole range for a longer view. This chart has only bars, not classified
trades, so its buy and sell split takes each bar's direction as its side; a footprint
is the place for real bid and ask volume.
